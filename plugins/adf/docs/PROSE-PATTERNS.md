# Prose patterns

The patterns to cut from text that people outside the team read: a requester's update, an end-user
page, a user-facing doc, a public issue. Each one makes the text longer without telling the
reader anything, and readers who learn to spot it as machine-written start skimming.

The skills that write for people run this pass on a draft before they show it. The patterns hold in
any language; the examples are English.

## The pass

1. Read the draft once against each table below.
2. Cut the pattern; don't reword around it. A sentence with nothing left said nothing.
3. Cut words, not facts. Every fact the draft gave is still there after the pass: a limit, a step,
   something the reader no longer has to do. If a cut took one with it, put the fact back, plainly.
4. Keep what the destination does on purpose: a template's fixed headings and labels (an issue's
   **What happens** and **Seen in**), the voice of the page or the docs being updated, and the
   project's own conventions win over this list.
5. The draft comes out shorter. A pass that adds a sentence went wrong.

## Openings and closings

| Pattern | Looks like | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Throat-clearing | "This update covers…", "In this section, we'll look at…", "I wanted to let you know that…" | Start with the point |
| Wrap-up | "In short", "Overall", "To sum up", a last paragraph that repeats the body | Stop when the content does |
| Offers and reassurance | "Let me know if you have any questions", "Hope this helps", "Rest assured" | Nothing; ask a question only when you need its answer |

## Sentences

| Pattern | Looks like | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Not X but Y" | "This isn't just a fix — it's an improvement", "It's not about speed, it's about trust" | Say Y |
| Hedges on known behavior | "may", "might", "should", "generally", "typically" about behavior that was checked | State it; hedge only what is uncertain, and say what |
| Puffery | "seamless", "robust", "comprehensive", "powerful", "significantly", "leverage", "streamline", "enhance" | The fact behind it — a number, a behavior — or nothing |
| Vague success | "successfully implemented", "fully tested", "improved performance" | What was done, and what was checked |
| Signposts | "It's worth noting that", "Importantly,", "Note that", "Here's the thing:" | The fact on its own |
| Saying it twice | A sentence that restates the one before it, or the heading above it | Once |

## Structure

| Pattern | Looks like | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| A bold lead-in on every bullet | "**Speed:** …", "**Security:** …", "**Design:** …" down the list | Plain bullets; bold for the one thing a skimming reader must not miss |
| Groups of three | "fast, reliable, and secure"; three parallel clauses in a row | What is there, whether one thing or two |
| Structure the content doesn't need | Headings in a short message, a list of one, a table of one or two rows | A sentence or a paragraph |
| Emoji and exclamation marks | "🚀 It's live!" | Plain text, unless the destination's voice uses them |
